## Noki (English)

> Noki is RIVIA&CO.’s English-language guide for people who want to travel slowly in small-town Japan, live here part of the year, work remotely or buy an akiya (vacant house).

- [Noki](https://rivia-co.com/noki): Plain guides to longer trips, living part of the year and buying an akiya in small-town Japan, from the people who run a place to stay in Shimoda.
- [Izu Peninsula](https://rivia-co.com/noki/izu-peninsula.html): The Izu Peninsula is a mountainous peninsula south-west of Tokyo with beaches on the coast and onsen towns inland. Where to base yourself and how to get there.
- [Kinmedai: Japan’s golden-eye snapper and where to eat it](https://rivia-co.com/noki/guides/kinmedai.html): Kinmedai is a red, golden-eyed deep-sea fish, often simmered as nitsuke. It’s fattiest around June, and the port of Shimoda in Izu is a good place to try it.
- [Buying an akiya in Japan? Live in one for a month first](https://rivia-co.com/noki/guides/akiya-try-before-you-buy.html): Japan has nine million empty homes. Non-residents can buy one, reporting it to the Ministry of Finance since April 2026. A month in the town tells you more.
- [Izu Peninsula from Tokyo: trains, fares and why to stay a week](https://rivia-co.com/noki/guides/izu-peninsula-from-tokyo.html): Direct trains reach Shimoda from Tokyo in about 2½ hours, for about ¥6,660 with a reserved seat. The Japan Rail Pass covers JR to Ito; beyond, you pay Izukyu.
- [Japan’s digital nomad visa, and where to base yourself](https://rivia-co.com/noki/guides/japan-digital-nomad-visa.html): Japan’s digital nomad visa gives remote workers earning ¥10 million a year up to six months, with no extension or residence card, so where you live matters.
- [Monthly rentals in Japan: how to rent a place for a month](https://rivia-co.com/noki/guides/monthly-rental-japan.html): A month in Japan usually means a monthly apartment or a licensed holiday rental, not an ordinary lease. What’s included, and what to ask first.
