Izu Peninsula from Tokyo: trains, fares and why to stay a week

The south of the Izu Peninsula is too far for a day and just right for a week. Routes, fares, seasons and the drawbacks nobody mentions.

By the Okaeri Japan team · Published 14 November 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  • Shimoda is about 2 hours 35 minutes from Tokyo by direct limited express. That is more than five hours of train for a day trip.
  • Shuzenji is about two hours away, by direct limited express or by Shinkansen to Mishima and a 35-minute local line.
  • The official swimming season runs from mid-July to the end of August. Lifeguards work only in that window.
  • Buses are infrequent and many shops close early outside summer. A house with a kitchen solves most of that.

How do you get to the Izu Peninsula from Tokyo?

The direct Odoriko limited express runs from Tokyo down the east coast of the Izu Peninsula to Shimoda, and a second branch runs inland to Shuzenji. These are the times and one-way fares to the four main stops.

From Tokyo toRouteTimeOne-way fare
AtamiOdoriko limited expressabout 80 minabout ¥3,500
ItoOdoriko limited expressabout 100 minabout ¥4,000
ShimodaOdoriko limited express, directabout 2 h 35 min¥6,660 reserved seat
ShuzenjiOdoriko limited express, directabout 2 habout ¥4,500

Times and fares for Atami, Ito and Shuzenji are from japan-guide.com. The Shimoda time and fare are from Ekitan. Fares change, so check before you book.

Shuzenji has a second route that runs far more often: take the Shinkansen to Mishima, then the Izuhakone Railway for 35 minutes (japan-guide.com). The Japan Rail Pass does not cover the private line between Ito and Shimoda, and the Mishima to Shuzenji section carries a ¥550 supplement (japan-guide.com).

Is the Izu Peninsula a day trip from Tokyo?

The north is. The south is not. The direct Odoriko limited express takes about 2 hours 35 minutes from Tokyo to Izukyu-Shimoda (Ekitan). Out and back is more than five hours on a train, for a few hours on a beach. The closer towns of Atami and Ito work as day trips. The south coast and the inland onsen towns reward people who stay.

Which Izu town suits you?

  • Shimoda is a port town with white-sand beaches. Shirahama Beach is about 15 minutes by bus from the station (JNTO). The harbour lands more kinmedai than anywhere in Japan.
  • Shuzenji is an inland hot-spring town built along a river, with a bamboo grove, a temple and a free public foot bath (Japan Uncharted).
  • Atami and Ito are the closest to Tokyo and have the most hotels. They are the busiest too.

When is the best time to visit the Izu Peninsula?

The official swimming season at Izu beaches runs from around mid-July to late August (japan-guide.com). In good weather the sea is pleasant from late May through September (japan-guide.com), but lifeguards are on duty only during the official season (FUN! JAPAN). Outside summer the towns are much quieter, and many restaurants and shops close early (The Digital Nomad Asia).

What changes when you rent a house in Izu?

A hotel room gives you one or two nights and a dinner time. A house gives you a kitchen, a washing machine and a table to work at, which is what makes four nights or a week practical. The usual rhythm is work in the morning, beach or bath in the afternoon, and dinner at home when the restaurants have closed.

Our home in Shimoda, NODE Shimoda, was set up for exactly this. It has a work desk, a washing machine and dryer, a kitchen with proper cooking equipment and self check-in with a smart lock. It is about ten minutes on foot from Izukyu-Shimoda Station, with restaurants, a convenience store and a supermarket within walking distance.

What are the drawbacks of staying in Izu?

Public transport is limited. Buses run infrequently, ride-hailing is not comparable to a big city, and the direct trains from Tokyo run mainly in the morning and early afternoon (The Digital Nomad Asia). Plan the day around the timetable, shop before the evening, and consider an e-bike or a rental car if you want to move between beaches.

Common questions

Can I use the Japan Rail Pass to get to Izu?

Partly. It covers the JR sections, but not the Izukyu line between Ito and Shimoda. Mishima to Shuzenji needs a ¥550 supplement (japan-guide.com).

Do I need a car on the Izu Peninsula?

Not to reach Shimoda or Shuzenji. Getting between beaches is slower without one.

How long does it take to get from Tokyo to Shimoda?

About 2 hours 35 minutes by the direct Odoriko limited express from Tokyo to Izukyu-Shimoda Station (Ekitan).
