Buying an akiya in Japan? Live in one for a month first

Japan has about nine million vacant homes and no rule against foreigners buying them. The town matters more than the house, and a month is enough to find out.

What is an akiya?

An akiya (空き家) is a house in Japan that nobody lives in. Japan’s 2023 Housing and Land Survey counted about 9.0 million vacant homes on 1 October 2023, or 13.8% of the housing stock (Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism). About 3.85 million of those were neither for rent or sale nor used as second homes, which is the group people usually mean when they talk about abandoned houses (Data Max).

Can foreigners buy an akiya?

Yes. Japan places no nationality or residency requirement on buying property (Housing Japan). The catch is on the immigration side: buying a home does not entitle you to a residence visa (Real Estate Japan). You can own the house and still be limited to the stay your passport allows.

Why live in the town before buying an akiya?

The house is the cheap part. The commitment is the town. A listing tells you the price and the floor plan. It does not tell you any of these:

  • How cold an old wooden house is in January, and what heating it costs you.
  • Whether daily life works without a car.
  • Where you buy food, and what time the shops close.
  • How rubbish sorting and collection days work on that street.
  • Whether your internet connection holds up for a working day.
  • What the humid months and typhoon season do to the building.
  • How the neighbours and the neighbourhood association treat a newcomer.

You can learn all seven in a month. You cannot learn any of them in a viewing.

How long can you stay in Japan to test a town?

Visitors from visa-exempt countries can generally stay up to 90 days as short-term visitors. Check the rule for your own passport with Japan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs before you plan.

Remote workers have a second route. Japan’s Digital Nomad status allows a stay of six months. It cannot be renewed, and it requires annual income of at least 10 million yen and private medical insurance (Immigration Services Agency).

What does a trial month in an akiya town look like?

  1. Week one: settle. Find the supermarket, the clinic, the bus stop. Work out the rubbish calendar.
  2. Week two: live normally. Work your usual hours. Cook. Notice what annoys you.
  3. Week three: ask. Visit the city hall desk that handles vacant houses. Talk to a local agent. Ask neighbours what they would fix first.
  4. Week four: decide. Write down what you would need to change about the house and about your own habits.

Where does Okaeri Japan come in?

We look after homes in small towns in Shizuoka and open them to guests for a week, a month or longer. Staying in one is a way to test a town without signing anything. We do not sell houses or act as agents.

The first, NODE Shimoda, is in the old town of Shimoda, on the Izu Peninsula: an older house renovated for long stays and licensed as a lodging under Japan’s Hotel Business Act, so it can take guests all year. Common questions

Is a cheap house in Japan really cheap?

The purchase price is one line of the bill. Renovation, taxes, insurance and upkeep are the others, and they vary with the building. Get written quotes before you commit.

Does owning a house in Japan help me get a visa?

No. Property ownership and immigration status are separate in Japan (Real Estate Japan).

How many akiya are there in Japan?

About 9.0 million homes were vacant on 1 October 2023, which is 13.8% of all housing, according to Japan’s 2023 Housing and Land Survey (Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism).
