Buying an akiya in Japan? Live in one for a month first

Japan has about nine million vacant homes and no rule against foreigners buying them. The town matters more than the house, and a month is enough to find out.

By the Okaeri Japan team · Updated 15 November 2026 · 4 min read

In short

  • An akiya is a vacant house in Japan. There were about 9.0 million of them, 13.8% of all homes, on 1 October 2023.
  • Foreigners can buy property in Japan without residency. Owning a house does not give you a visa.
  • A month in a town shows what a listing cannot: winter cold, driving, shopping and neighbours.
  • Staying by the week or month in a renovated older house is a way to test a town before you sign anything.

An akiya (空き家) is a house in Japan that nobody lives in. Japan’s 2023 Housing and Land Survey counted about 9.0 million vacant homes on 1 October 2023, or 13.8% of the housing stock (Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism). About 3.85 million of those were neither for rent or sale nor used as second homes, which is the group people usually mean when they talk about abandoned houses (Data Max).

Can foreigners buy an akiya?

Yes. Japan places no nationality or residency requirement on buying property (Housing Japan). The catch is on the immigration side: buying a home does not entitle you to a residence visa (Real Estate Japan). You can own the house and still be limited to the stay your passport allows.

Why live in the town before buying an akiya?

The house is the cheap part. The commitment is the town. A listing tells you the price and the floor plan. It does not tell you any of these, and a month in the town does:

What to find outHow a month shows you
How cold an old wooden house is in January, and what heating costsStay through a cold spell and watch the electricity or fuel you use.
Whether daily life works without a carDo a week of shopping and errands on foot, bus and bicycle.
Where you buy food, and when the shops closeCook most of your meals and note what you could not find.
How rubbish sorting and collection work on that streetFollow the local calendar for a month. Ask city hall for the English version if there is one.
Whether the internet holds up for a working dayWork your normal hours, including video calls.
What humid months and typhoons do to the buildingAsk neighbours and local builders what fails first in houses like it.
How neighbours and the neighbourhood association treat a newcomerSay hello, join a clean-up day if there is one, and ask how newcomers usually fit in.

You cannot learn any of these in a viewing.

How long can you stay in Japan to test a town?

Most visa-exempt nationalities can stay up to 90 days as short-term visitors, but a few get 15 or 30 days, and some passports have extra conditions (Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan). Check the rule for your own passport before you plan.

Remote workers have a second route. Japan’s Digital Nomad status allows a stay of six months. It cannot be renewed, and it requires annual income of at least 10 million yen and private medical insurance (Immigration Services Agency).

What does a trial month in an akiya town look like?

  1. Week one: settle. Find the supermarket, the clinic, the bus stop. Work out the rubbish calendar.
  2. Week two: live normally. Work your usual hours. Cook. Notice what annoys you.
  3. Week three: ask. Visit the city hall desk that handles vacant houses. Talk to a local agent. Ask neighbours what they would fix first.
  4. Week four: decide. Write down what you would need to change about the house and about your own habits.

Can you stay in a renovated older house before buying one?

Yes. Renting an older house that has already been renovated for long stays shows you what living in one is like, without signing anything. A town within reach of Tokyo is easier to test: the Izu Peninsula, for example, is two to three hours away, as our guide to getting to the Izu Peninsula from Tokyo explains.

Our home, NODE Shimoda, is in the old town of Shimoda, on the Izu Peninsula: an older house renovated for long stays and licensed as a lodging under Japan’s Hotel Business Act, so it can take guests all year. Longer stays get weekly and monthly discounts. Most guests so far have come from Europe and North America, many of them working remotely.

Common questions

Is a cheap house in Japan really cheap?

The purchase price is one line of the bill. Renovation, taxes, insurance and upkeep are the others, and they vary with the building. Get written quotes before you commit.

Does owning a house in Japan help me get a visa?

No. Property ownership and immigration status are separate in Japan (Real Estate Japan).

How many akiya are there in Japan?

About 9.0 million homes were vacant on 1 October 2023, which is 13.8% of all housing, according to Japan’s 2023 Housing and Land Survey (Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism).
