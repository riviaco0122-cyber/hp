Izu Peninsula from Tokyo: trains, fares and why to stay a week

The south of the Izu Peninsula is too far for a day trip and just right for a week. Here’s how to get there, when to go, and the downsides nobody mentions.

By the Noki team · Updated 15 November 2026 · 4 min read

In short

  • Shimoda is about 2 hours 35 minutes from Tokyo on the direct limited express, so a day trip means more than five hours on the train.
  • Shuzenji is about two hours away, either direct or by Shinkansen to Mishima and a 35-minute local line.
  • The official swimming season runs from mid-July to late August, and that’s the only time lifeguards are on duty.
  • Buses are few and many shops close early outside summer, which is why a house with a kitchen makes such a difference.

How do you get to the Izu Peninsula from Tokyo?

The easiest way is the Odoriko limited express, which runs direct from Tokyo down the east coast of the Izu Peninsula to Shimoda. A second branch heads inland to Shuzenji. Here are the times and one-way fares to the four main stops.

From Tokyo toRouteTimeOne-way fare
AtamiOdoriko limited expressabout 80 minabout ¥3,500
ItoOdoriko limited expressabout 100 minabout ¥4,000
ShimodaOdoriko limited express, directabout 2 h 35 min¥6,660 reserved seat
ShuzenjiOdoriko limited express, directabout 2 habout ¥4,500

Times and fares for Atami, Ito and Shuzenji are from japan-guide.com. The Shimoda time and fare are from Ekitan. Fares change, so check before you book.

For Shuzenji there’s also a route with far more trains: the Shinkansen to Mishima, then 35 minutes on the Izuhakone Railway (japan-guide.com). If you’re travelling on a Japan Rail Pass, it won’t cover the private line between Ito and Shimoda, and the Mishima to Shuzenji section carries a ¥550 supplement (japan-guide.com).

Is the Izu Peninsula a day trip from Tokyo?

The north is, but the south really isn’t. The direct Odoriko takes about 2 hours 35 minutes from Tokyo to Izukyu-Shimoda (Ekitan), so going there and back means more than five hours on a train for a few hours on the beach. Atami and Ito, closer to Tokyo, work fine as day trips. The south coast and the inland hot-spring towns are far better if you stay.

Which Izu town suits you?

It mostly comes down to how far you want to go and how long you can stay.

TownFrom TokyoWhat it’s likeDay trip or stay?
Atami and ItoAbout 80 to 100 minThe closest to Tokyo and the busiest, with the most hotels.A day trip works
ShuzenjiAbout 2 hA hot-spring town inland, along a river, with a bamboo grove, a temple and a free public foot bath (Japan Uncharted).Better to stay
ShimodaAbout 2 h 35 minA port town with white-sand beaches. Shirahama Beach is about 15 minutes by bus from the station (JNTO). The city and the prefecture describe its harbour as Japan’s top landing port for kinmedai (Shizuoka Prefecture).Better to stay

When is the best time to visit the Izu Peninsula?

For swimming, it’s mid-July to late August. For a quieter stay, the months either side are hard to beat.

WhenWhat to expect
Mid-July to late AugustThe official swimming season at Izu beaches (japan-guide.com), and the only time lifeguards are on duty (FUN! JAPAN).
Late May to SeptemberIn good weather the sea is pleasant (japan-guide.com), and outside the school holidays there are fewer people around.
The rest of the yearThe towns get much quieter, and plenty of restaurants and shops close early (The Digital Nomad Asia).

What changes when you rent a house in Izu?

A hotel room is built around one or two nights and a set dinner time. A house gives you a kitchen, a washing machine and a table to work at, and that’s what makes four nights or a week easy. A typical day ends up looking like this: work in the morning, the beach or a hot spring in the afternoon, and dinner at home once the restaurants have closed.

Our home in Shimoda, NODE Shimoda, was set up for exactly this kind of stay. It has a work desk, a washing machine and dryer, a properly equipped kitchen and self check-in with a smart lock. It’s about ten minutes on foot from Izukyu-Shimoda Station, with restaurants, a convenience store and a supermarket close by.

What are the downsides of staying in Izu?

Getting around takes a bit of planning. Buses don’t run often, ride-hailing is nothing like a big city, and the direct trains from Tokyo run mainly in the morning and early afternoon (The Digital Nomad Asia). It helps to plan the day around the timetable and get your shopping done before evening. If you want to hop between beaches, an e-bike or a rental car makes life much easier.

Common questions

Can I use the Japan Rail Pass to get to Izu?

Partly. It covers the JR sections, but not the Izukyu line between Ito and Shimoda, and Mishima to Shuzenji needs a ¥550 supplement (japan-guide.com).

Do I need a car on the Izu Peninsula?

Not to get to Shimoda or Shuzenji. You’ll only really miss one when you want to move between beaches.

How long does it take to get from Tokyo to Shimoda?

About 2 hours 35 minutes on the direct Odoriko limited express from Tokyo to Izukyu-Shimoda Station (Ekitan).
