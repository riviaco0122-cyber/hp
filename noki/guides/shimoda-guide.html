Shimoda, Japan: a guide for a week or longer

Shimoda is a small port town at the southern tip of the Izu Peninsula. In a week, you can pick beaches by mood, cook local fish and settle in.

By the Noki team · Published 20 November 2026 · 11 min read

In short

  • Pick a beach by what you want: calm coves for children, surf beaches, or a quieter stretch of sand.
  • The old town is easy on foot: the ropeway, port and Perry Road are within 15 minutes of the station.
  • Outside summer it’s quieter, with daffodils in winter, the Black Ship Festival in May and hydrangeas in June.

Is Shimoda worth a week?

We think so, if you want more than one beach and a quick look at the station area. A night is enough to swim once and eat well. A week lets you try different beaches, find a café you like and fall into the town’s slower rhythm.

Shimoda sits at the southern tip of the Izu Peninsula, and it’s a small town of about 18,400 people (November 2026)1. The coastline, though, runs for around 47 kilometres2, so there’s far more shore to explore than the size suggests.

This is also where Japan first opened its doors to America. Under the 1854 Treaty of Kanagawa, Shimoda became one of the country’s first two open ports3, and you’ll keep bumping into that story as you walk the old town.

The train ride from Tokyo is long enough that you’ll want to stay put once you’re here. We’ve covered trains, fares and bus passes in our guide to getting to Izu from Tokyo. If you have more than a week, we’d spend most of it in Shimoda and add a night or two elsewhere on the peninsula.

Which Shimoda beach suits you?

It depends on what you want from the day, because Shimoda’s nine official beaches are surprisingly different. Some are sheltered coves where small children can paddle, and others are known for surfing.

Nabetahama (130 m)The beach closest to town, in an inlet with hardly any waves. There’s no car park or shower4.A quick swim, small childrenBus from stand 3 or 4, about 7 min, then 5 min on foot4
Sotoura (470 m)A cove with shallow, calm water, popular with families. People sea-kayak further out. No surfing area5.Children, calm swimmingBus from stand 9, about 10 min, then 3 min on foot5
Shirahama Chuo (720 m)The quiet north end of the Shirahama coast, with rock pools. It’s the only beach in Shimoda where you can barbecue6.Families, rock poolsBus from stand 9, about 18 min, then 1 min on foot6
Shirahama Ohama (about 700 m)One of the largest beaches in Izu, by the main coast road and busy with surfers and young people7,8.A lively day, surfingBus from stand 9, about 15 min8
Tatadohama (450 m)Well known for surfing and bodyboarding, with national contests. It has warm showers you can use all year9.SurfingBus from stand 3 or 4, about 7 min, then 3 min on foot9
Kisami Ohama (770 m)A big beach away from the main road, so it feels quieter. Surfers and families share it, and there are cafés nearby10.A long, quieter dayBus from stand 3 or 4, about 10 min, then 8 min on foot10

Beach lengths are the tourism association’s figures; for Shirahama Ohama, the city’s list. The “stand” is the numbered bus stop outside the station.

That leaves three more. Iritahama is a calm beach lined with cycad trees, and surfers use it too11. Kujuppama is a 50-metre cove next to the Suzaki Imperial Villa, with rock pools on both sides and no large tents allowed12. Toji is rarely crowded and close to the Ryugu sea cave, but only four or five buses a day go there13, so it’s a beach you plan your day around.

Wherever you end up, the water’s clean. In Shizuoka Prefecture’s 2026 survey, all nine beaches got the top AA grade14. Six of them (every beach in the table except Nabetahama, plus Iritahama) also hold a safety certification from the Japan Lifesaving Association7.

Lifeguards are only on duty during the official season, which the city sets each year15. Our Izu travel guide has the 2026 dates. The season also comes with a few rules that are worth knowing before you pack16:

  • No tents, though sunshades are fine.
  • No open fires or barbecues, except in part of Shirahama Chuo.
  • No fireworks and no overnight camping.
  • No amplified speakers, a rule added in 202516.

What can you do in Shimoda besides the beach?

Quite a lot, and most of it is within a short walk of the station. On a day off the sand, we’d start high up, wander the old town and finish in a hot bath.

The Shimoda Ropeway station is a one-minute walk from Izukyu-Shimoda Station. From the top of Mt Nesugata you can see the town, the port, the Izu Islands and the Amagi mountains17. Back down, the port and the Michi-no-Eki, a roadside market building, are about 10 minutes away on foot18.

For the history, head to Perry Road and Ryosenji temple, about 15 minutes from the station19,20. Perry Road follows the route Perry’s men marched to the temple. Today it’s about 500 metres of stone paving beside a small stream3,19, and we think it’s one of the nicest short walks in town. Later in 1854, the follow-up Shimoda Treaty was signed in Ryosenji’s main hall3. If you’d like to go further, Gyokusenji temple housed Japan’s first US consulate, opened by Townsend Harris in 185621,22.

To end the day in hot water, eight hotels and inns open their baths to day visitors23. Adults pay between ¥600 and ¥1,500 (as of November 2026). Each place opens at its own time, somewhere between 10:00 and 15:00, and they may turn you away when they’re busy23. If you just want to warm your feet, the Kaikoku-no-yu footbath in front of the station is open from 9:00 to 17:0023.

Rain doesn’t have to ruin the day. The Michi-no-Eki has a harbour museum about the town’s history on its fourth floor18, and Ryosenji has a treasure hall20. On a dry day, the Wakanoura promenade takes you 1.2 kilometres along the coast in about 15 minutes24. For something longer, the paths round Shimoda Park take about an hour and a half24.

Is Shimoda worth visiting outside summer?

Yes. The beaches go quiet, but there’s something on in most seasons. It’s mild, too: the city puts the average yearly temperature at about 17°C2.

WhenWhat’s on
20 December to 31 JanuaryThe daffodil festival at Tsumekizaki, with up to about 3 million flowers, usually at their best in January. The bus from stand 10 outside the station takes about 20 minutes25.
Winter into early springCamellias in Shimoda Park, with about 160 garden varieties26. Cherry blossoms follow from mid-March to early April27.
MayThe Black Ship Festival, held since 1934. In 2026 it ran from 15 to 17 May, with fireworks over the port, a US–Japan parade and a re-enactment of the Shimoda Treaty at Ryosenji28.
JuneThe hydrangea festival in Shimoda Park, with about 3 million blooms of over 100 varieties (1 to 30 June in 2026). The park is hilly, and carts are on hand for people who find walking hard29.
July and AugustBeach season. In 2026 there were fireworks at Shirahama on 25 July, and the old town’s Taiko Matsuri drum festival is held on 14 and 15 August27.
AutumnHand-held te-zutsu fireworks (10 October in 2026), the Shirahama shrine festival in late October, and the start of the spiny lobster (ise-ebi) season, which runs to early May27.

Summer is when the town fills up, and in our experience it’s also when more of our guests stay for longer. If you’d rather walk, soak and eat well than swim, we’d pick the cooler months.

How do you shop and cook for a week in Shimoda?

Our advice is to buy your fish at the port and cook it yourself, which is half the fun of having a kitchen. The Izu Fisheries Cooperative shop is on the ground floor of the Michi-no-Eki Kaikoku Shimoda Minato. It’s where you’ll find kinmedai, live spiny lobster, turban shells and other seafood30,31. It’s open from 8:00 to 16:30 with no fixed closing day (as of April 2025)30. The JA farmers’ shop in the same building has local vegetables, wasabi and products made from New Summer oranges31.

If you’d rather have someone else cook the town’s famous red fish, our guide to kinmedai covers where to eat it. For everyday shopping, there are supermarkets and convenience stores in town, so a kitchen makes a week much cheaper and more relaxed.

Rubbish takes a little learning. Burnable rubbish goes out twice a week in city bags32. Cans, bottles, PET bottles and paper are collected separately, twice a month32. Every place has its own system, though, so it’s easiest to ask your host when you arrive. Here’s what else we’d do on day one:

  1. Find the nearest supermarket and the fish shop at the Michi-no-Eki.
  2. Ask your host how to sort rubbish and where it goes.
  3. Pick up a map at the tourism information centre near the station, open from 9:00 to 16:4533.
  4. Look up the bus times for the beaches you want, since some routes run only a few times a day.
  5. Find the nearest high ground from where you’re staying (see the next section).

What should you know about tsunamis in Shimoda?

If you feel a strong earthquake near the coast, go to high ground straight away, without waiting for a warning34. Shimoda faces the open sea, and in 1854 the Ansei tsunami swept Shimoda Bay and destroyed most of the town’s houses35.

Shizuoka Prefecture’s 2013 estimate looked at the worst case, which it describes as extremely unlikely, and put the highest possible tsunami in Shimoda at 33 metres36. It isn’t a forecast, but it’s why the advice is to get up high, fast. Japan’s weather agency, JMA, aims to issue alerts about three minutes after an earthquake34.

AlertExpected waveWhat to do
Tsunami advisory0.2 to 1 mGet out of the water and away from the shore34
Tsunami warning1 to 3 mMove to high ground or an evacuation building at once34
Major tsunami warningOver 3 mMove to high ground or an evacuation building at once34

Tsunamis come in several waves, so JMA advises you to stay on high ground until the warning or advisory is lifted34. The city publishes hazard and evacuation maps, though only in Japanese37. It has also named a few tsunami evacuation buildings38, and it runs an online map that shows the height above sea level of any spot you tap39.

The Japan Tourism Agency’s free app, Safety tips, sends earthquake, tsunami and weather warnings in English and other languages40. We’d install it before you arrive. In late July 2025, there was a tsunami warning for the Shizuoka coast, later downgraded to an advisory41,42. As things changed, we messaged our overseas guests to tell them what was happening and that they needed to evacuate. Guests thanked us afterwards.

Where should you stay in Shimoda for a week?

Without a car, we’d stay in the old town near the station. Shops, the port and every bus route are close by. If the beach is the whole point of your trip, staying near it saves you the bus rides.

AreaSuits you if…Keep in mind
Old town, near the stationYou want to walk to shops, the port and Perry Road, and reach any beach by bus.You’ll need a bus or a bike for most beaches.
ShirahamaYou want the big beaches on your doorstep.It’s about 15 minutes by bus from town8, so evenings out mean a ride back.
Southern beaches (Tatado, Irita, Kisami)You’re here to surf or want a quieter beach.They’re 7 to 10 minutes by bus from the station9,10, plus a short walk.

Wherever you stay, a bike makes the week much easier. The tourism association rents bikes and e-bikes at the Michi-no-Eki, from ¥500 for an hour or ¥2,000 for a day43. Rentals run from 9:00 to 17:00. You don’t need to book, but you’ll need ID such as a passport43.

We run NODE Shimoda, an apartment of about 40 m² in the old town for up to four guests. It has a kitchen, a washing machine and gas dryer, and a work desk. It’s on the first floor, up about 13 steps, with a shower but no bath44. It’s an eight-minute walk on the flat from the station45. The supermarket is three minutes away, and Perry Road five. One guest who stayed in January wrote that the sea was close by, they could see the year’s first sunrise, and the supermarket was handy.

Common questions

Is Shimoda worth visiting?

Yes, especially for a week. It’s a small port town at the tip of Izu with nine official beaches, a walkable old town and seasonal festivals1,16,27. A week lets you try several beaches and live at the town’s pace.

What is the best beach in Shimoda?

It depends on what you want. Sotoura and Nabetahama are calm coves that suit children4,5. Tatadohama is especially well known among surfers9, and Kisami Ohama is big and quieter10.

Can you swim in Shimoda outside summer?

Lifeguards only work during the official season, which the city sets each year, so outside it you swim without them15. Tatadohama has warm showers you can use all year9.

What is there to do in Shimoda in winter?

The daffodil festival at Tsumekizaki runs from 20 December to 31 January25, and camellias bloom in Shimoda Park26. Day hot springs, the ropeway and coastal walks fill the rest of the week17,23,24.

What should I do if there’s a tsunami warning in Shimoda?

Move to high ground or an evacuation building at once, and stay until the warning is lifted. If you feel strong shaking near the coast, go without waiting for a warning34. The free Safety tips app sends alerts in English40.
